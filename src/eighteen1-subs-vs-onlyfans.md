# Subs vs OnlyFans for Creators: What's Different (2026)

Subs has been getting a lot of attention since actress Megan Fox announced on 9 October 2026 that she had become a co-owner of the platform and launched her own page there. If you're a creator, you might be asking a simpler question: is Subs actually different from OnlyFans, and is it worth joining?

This guide compares the two on the things that matter day to day: what you earn, what you can post, how fans find you and how they pay. Platform terms change often, so treat this as a starting point and check each platform's current terms before you decide.

## A quick background

**OnlyFans** has been around since 2016 and is the best-known subscription platform for creators, especially adult creators.

**Subs** (subs.com) launched in May 2025. It was founded by Tim Stokely, who also co-founded OnlyFans. Subs describes itself as a mobile-first subscription platform for all kinds of creators, from podcasters and athletes to musicians, influencers and adult creators, combining video hosting, paid subscriptions and one-to-one interaction.

So the two platforms share some history, but they're separate companies with separate rules.

## Payouts and fees

On the headline number, the two platforms are the same. Both keep 20% and pay creators 80%. On OnlyFans that covers subscriptions, paid messages and tips. Subs says its 80% includes subscriptions, pay-per-view, tips and video or audio calls, and its terms state the split is calculated excluding VAT.

The differences are in the details of getting paid:

- **Minimum withdrawal.** OnlyFans says the minimum depends on your payout method and is $20 for most. Subs sets a $50 minimum.
- **Timing.** OnlyFans holds each payment in a pending balance for 7 days (21 days at first in a small number of countries with high chargeback rates), then payouts usually arrive in 3 to 5 business days. Subs says payouts usually take 7 to 10 business days to appear in your bank account.
- **Payout methods.** OnlyFans says the methods available depend on where you live. Subs pays to a verified bank account in your name through its payout processors. Check both platforms' current options for your country.
- **Chargebacks.** Both can deduct your share if a fan gets a refund or chargeback.
- **Agents.** Subs' terms allow an agreed management fee to be deducted and paid directly to your agent, which may matter if you work with an agency.

If you're just starting out and cash flow matters, the lower minimum on OnlyFans is worth noting. For established creators, the 80/20 split being the same means the decision usually comes down to other things.

## Content rules

Both platforms allow adult content from consenting adults, and both ban illegal, non-consensual and underage content. The big difference is where explicit content can appear.

**OnlyFans** keeps sexually explicit images and videos behind a paywall. Fans need a verified account and a payment card to see photo or video content.

**Subs** allows adult content, but its terms say sexually explicit content can't go in:

- public posts,
- follower posts (free content for followers), or
- subscriber posts tied to your general subscription.

Explicit content is allowed in direct, group or broadcast messages, pay-per-view content, calls and other paid private content that isn't on your main page or feed. In practice, that means your Subs feed works more like a brand-friendly showcase, with explicit content sold privately.

Subs also has specific rules on AI content: it's only allowed if it clearly features you alone and is labelled, for example with #ai. Deepfakes are banned outright. Profile pictures and banners can't contain nudity.

If your current business is built on an explicit subscription feed, this is the most important difference to understand before you move anything over.

## Discovery and audience

This is where the platforms really differ.

**OnlyFans** has no traditional search. In its own words, you can only find a creator on the platform by using their exact handle. Fans find you on other platforms first and then follow you to OnlyFans. That's why promotion on X, TikTok, Instagram and Reddit matters so much to OnlyFans creators.

**Subs** has an Explore section described as "where discovery begins", plus a Shows section. Only public content appears there. Because explicit content can't be public on Subs, discovery there depends on your non-explicit content.

Subs is also clearly courting celebrities and public figures from film, sport and music, which may bring in new fans over time. How much discovery traffic smaller creators actually get is hard to know yet, so treat it as a possible bonus, not a replacement for your own promotion.

## Payments and fan experience

**OnlyFans** fans pay with a debit or credit card through OnlyFans' own checkout.

**Subs** lists Visa, Mastercard, Apple Pay and Google Pay on supported devices. Its help center is inconsistent about American Express and Discover, so check their current terms if your fans use those. Both platforms charge in US dollars, so fans outside the US may pay currency conversion fees.

Apple Pay and Google Pay can make paying faster, especially on a phone. But they only help if the fan reaches the page in a browser where those options actually work, which brings us to the problem both platforms share.

## Which to choose, and can you use both?

There's no single right answer. A simple way to think about it:

- **OnlyFans** may suit you better if your income comes mainly from an explicit subscription feed, you already have an audience there, or you want the lower $20 withdrawal minimum.
- **Subs** may suit you better if you want a brand-friendly public presence with explicit content sold privately, you like the idea of built-in discovery, or your fans prefer Apple Pay or Google Pay.

You can also use both. Plenty of creators run more than one platform. If you do, check each platform's terms on exclusivity and cross-promotion first, keep your content plans clear, and make it easy for fans to choose. The easiest way to do that is one link that offers both.

## The promotion problem both platforms share

Whichever you pick, you still have to send fans there from social media, and that's where many creators lose people.

**Platforms flag adult links.** TikTok, Instagram and X read the link in your bio to build previews and check where it goes. A bio pointing straight at an adult platform can count against your account.

**In-app browsers break logins and payments.** Most fans tap your link inside the app's built-in browser. They aren't logged in there, saved passwords often don't fill in, and Apple Pay or Google Pay may not work. Many give up before subscribing, and you never see it.
